> Our own path is the same story in miniature: 0.02 (starter) → 1.37 (Duck + Qwen3.8-27B) → 3.59 (Flash-Next NVFP4) → 4.17 (sheetu) → 27.05 (Franzen's base). Each big step came from adopting someone else's model or serving stack. None of our own harness additions moved the LB beyond the draw-to-draw spread.
>
> Where the rest has to come from: in our 250 public-game save runs, the agent used a median of 0.71× the human's moves on the levels it cleared, and 62% of all its actions went into the one level it never cleared. Cleared levels already score at the 1.15 cap, so the remaining points are in levels it never figures out. So far, more tokens per game is what has bought those (see Tong Hui Kang's comparison of the M2 winners).
>
> One caution on the final number: the public LB is each team's best of many draws, while the private board scores 55 other games, and picking your best public draw doesn't pick your best private one. I'd expect top private scores to land below their public peaks, more so for teams with many submissions.

Evidence:
- 1.17 on 22 May is from `memo.md` (Kamradt on X). 27.29 and 9.96 are from `report-2026-09-27.md`. 50.65 and 27.89 are from `external_submission/index.csv` (snapshot `publicleaderboard-2026-10-01T11:43:58`). The 10 Oct figures are from the leaderboard CSV above.
- Our path comes from `submissions/index.csv` and the Kaggle submission list.
- The level efficiency figures come from `corpus/derive_level_efficiency_2026-10-10.py`, run over the 23 `benchmark.json` files in `submissions/` and `experiments/`: 250 game runs, 433 cleared levels, a median agent/human ratio of 0.706, and 62.3% of 34,942 actions spent on the uncleared level. These are public-demo save runs from the LB ~2.5–4 harness era, not hidden-set play.
- At a ratio of 0.71, (1/0.71)² = 1.98, which the formula caps at 1.15 (`docs.arcprize.org/methodology`).
